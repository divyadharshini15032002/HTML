A.P.J.Kalam

A.P.J. Abdul Kalam's history is defined by his journey from a humble background in Rameswaram to becoming a celebrated scientist and statesman who served as India's 11th President from 2002 to 2007. His scientific career focused on developing India's missile and nuclear programs, earning him the nickname "Missile Man," while his presidency was marked by his connection with the public, leading to the title "People's President". He was born on October 15, 1931, and passed away on July 27, 2015. Early life and education Born in a modest Muslim family in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, Kalam was the youngest of five siblings. Despite his family's financial struggles, he excelled in his studies. He earned a degree in physics from St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirapalli in 1954 and later graduated with a degree in aeronautical engineering from the Madras Institute of Technology. Scientific career

Kalam joined the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) in 1958. He moved to the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) in 1969, where he served as the project director for India's first satellite launch vehicle, the SLV-III. He is credited with a pivotal role in India's missile and nuclear weapons programs, leading projects that developed the Agni and Prithvi missiles. His leadership was also crucial in the Pokhran-II nuclear tests in 1998, which solidified his national and international acclaim. Presidency and later life Kalam was elected the 11th President of India in 2002. During his presidency, he was known for his accessibility and focus on education and youth empowerment, earning him the moniker "People's President". After his term, he continued to engage in public service, teaching, and writing. He passed away in 2015 while delivering a lecture at the Indian Institute of Management in Shillong.
